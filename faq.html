About Thangkas

What is a Thangka? ⌄

A Thangka is a Buddhist religious scroll painting originating from the Himalayas—primarily Nepal, Tibet, and Bhutan. Traditionally painted on canvas using mineral pigments, gold, and natural materials, each Thangka depicts a deity, mandala, or spiritual narrative.

Historically, Thangkas were portable religious objects for meditation and teaching, carried by traveling monks. Today, they remain sacred art pieces valued for their spiritual significance and extraordinary craftsmanship.

What materials are used in authentic Thangkas? ⌄

Traditional materials include:

  • Canvas/Cotton: Hand-prepared cotton canvas, often mounted on brocade silk
  • Pigments: Natural mineral pigments (lapis lazuli, gold, silver, ochre, malachite)
  • Gold: 24k gold leaf or filigree for embellishment
  • Binding: Silk brocade borders in traditional patterns

Synthetic materials were never used in authentic traditional pieces. We source only authentic, hand-prepared works.

How long does it take to paint a Thangka? ⌄

Depending on size and complexity, a Thangka can take anywhere from 3 months to 2+ years to complete. Master artists spend their entire lives perfecting their craft, with many works taking 6-12 months of dedicated work.

Our gallery features works from artists who have trained for 15-40+ years, ensuring each piece meets the highest standards of quality and spiritual integrity.

Sourcing & Authenticity

How do you verify authenticity? ⌄

Each piece in our collection is personally sourced and verified through:

  • Direct relationships with master artists in Kathmandu, Bhutan, and Tibet
  • Artist signatures and documentation of lineage and training
  • Analysis of materials, pigment aging, and canvas preparation
  • Certificates of authenticity and provenance records
  • Personal inspection by our curatorial team

We never purchase from unknown sellers and reject any piece with questionable origin.

Where are your Thangkas sourced from? ⌄

Our collection comes exclusively from:

  • Kathmandu, Nepal: Home to the largest community of master artists and traditional painters
  • Bhutan: Known for rigorous spiritual standards and preservation of classical techniques
  • Private collections: Rare, inherited pieces with documented histories

Every purchase directly supports living artists and their families, preserving an endangered cultural tradition.

Do you work with fair trade practices? ⌄

Yes. We believe artists deserve fair compensation for their years of training and labor. Our prices directly reflect artist fees, and we maintain ongoing relationships with creators to ensure their prosperity.

When you purchase a Thangka from us, you're investing directly in the artist's practice and cultural preservation.

Care & Preservation

How should I care for my Thangka? ⌄

Display & Storage:

  • Hang in a stable, climate-controlled space (avoid direct sunlight)
  • Maintain consistent temperature (65-75°F) and humidity (40-55%)
  • Keep away from heat sources and damp areas
  • If storing, roll gently with acid-free tissue and place in a wooden chest

Cleaning: Do not clean the painting itself. Dust gently with a soft, dry brush on the mounting brocade only.

What if my Thangka gets damaged? ⌄

For minor damage or concerns, contact us immediately. We recommend consulting a professional conservator experienced with Himalayan textiles.

In Kathmandu, there are specialized restoration studios that can address issues ranging from loose brocade to pigment restoration. We can provide referrals.

Will the colors fade over time? ⌄

Traditional mineral pigments and 24k gold are extremely stable. With proper display and storage, a Thangka can remain vibrant for centuries.

Slight aging is natural and adds to the spiritual authenticity of a piece. Many collectors value the patina of age.
